# Polo — brand voice

For typography, color hex codes, type scale, padding, and fixed-module visual specs, see the Figma assembly skill instead — this file is copy/story only.

## Sub-lines & casting
- **Polo Men**
- **Polo Women**
- **Polo Kids** — note: kids under ~2 years old are considered babies for these emails, and babies go with the plain **RL logotype** instead of the Polo logo.

## Overview
Polo is Ralph Lauren's most ubiquitous label, designed for the whole family (men, women, kids) — family is a running theme. Polo's tone is the most confident, editorialized, and culture-facing of any RL brand; more open to contemporary shorthand and trend language than the others. Polo inherits the Master Brand's Sophisticated/Concise/Romantic/Confident pillars (`master-brand.md`) but expresses them through its own register — see also `copy-voice.md` for Women's Polo's more recent voice evolution and worked email examples.

## The story
"More than just luxury — Polo represents a way of living." Rooted in Mr. Lauren's own account of the name: a sport with a sensibility that was "sporty and international... tweedy and sophisticated... stylish." Polo's world is street-style collectors and California surfers and New England prepsters and moms dressing their families — huge breadth, which is what makes it culturally ubiquitous. Less emphasis on design-inspiration storytelling than the luxury sub-brands (Collection/Purple Label) — more on lifestyle and emotional values.
- **Men's Polo**: authentic and iconic — the original symbol of the modern preppy lifestyle, blending Ivy League classics and English haberdashery with downtown and All-American sport.
- **Women's Polo**: personal expression for the modern woman — effortlessly romantic with chic sportiness, mixing all-American style with downtown edge.
- **Polo Kids**: reflects the same heritage and modern spirit as the adult lines, interpreted in sophisticated, vibrant colors; full size range from Baby/Infant through Toddler, Girls 16, Boys 20.

## Tone pillars
- **NOSTALGIC** — reflects back on good, old things that withstand the test of time. *"Denim feels like coming home again."*
- **INSPIRING** — especially for campaign storytelling: determination, love, and dreams that change the world for the better; optimistic. *"For our Olympic heroes, the journey continues."*
- **ELEVATED** — conveys luxury without being condescending or out of touch; noble and refined yet simple and clear.
- **GROUNDED** — approachable, easy to understand; no words that need a dictionary. References tradition/family/heritage with a modern twist; avoids anything that reads as a fleeting trend or "nowness."

**Do**: reference heritage with a modern twist, keep vocabulary accessible, lean into family/togetherness/sport themes.
**Don't** (flagged as tonally incorrect in the guide): forced puns or "code red! link in bio"-style urgency copy, overclaiming ("the most versatile piece in every man's closet"), leaning on generic sustainability buzzwords ("planet-friendly") without a vetted claim (see `sustainability-claims.md`).

## PFS / PRO (Polo Factory Store / Polo Retail Outlets) — Polo's outlet channel
Tonally distinct from mainline Polo — the most functional, product-focused, deal-oriented voice in the whole RL portfolio. Family is again a key theme. Tagline: *"To live the American Dream in Spirited Style."*
- **FRIENDLY** — warm, inviting, simple language; stories about happiness and simplicity.
- **RELAXED** — laid-back ease; never fussy, condescending, or over the top; neighborly.
- **DIRECT** — straightforward and earnest; say what you mean, nothing overdone or precious.
- **FUNCTIONAL** — many PFS customers drive ~30 miles to a store and there's no full e-commerce site, so email carries more of the product-information load than it does for other brands — simple but descriptive language about product benefits matters more here than anywhere else.

## Story best practices
- Emails from the same collection/campaign keep one consistent look and feel throughout — don't mix registers mid-campaign.
- Don't reuse the same image crop twice within one email — mix full-outfit shots with closer crops for rhythm, and use laydowns between on-figure photos where the image mapping offers them.

## Known fixed-shape modules for this brand
These have a set structure — when a brief calls for one, don't invent a different shape for it (see `module-types.md` for the general module catalog):
- **CYO (Create Your Own)** — copy goes directly below the picture, CTA directly below the copy; never split them (copy above the image, CTA below), unless the brief explicitly asks for that.
- **Social Module** — fixed H3 "Follow Us" + icon row + a closing line; copy is essentially fixed, not written fresh per campaign.
- **Store Locator Module** — fixed address-block shape; copy is store info, not brand storytelling.
