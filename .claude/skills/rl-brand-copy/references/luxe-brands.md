# Luxe Brands (Purple Label / Collection) — brand voice

For typography, color hex codes, type scale, padding, and fixed-module visual specs, see the Figma assembly skill instead — this file is copy/story only.

## Sub-lines
Check the marketing brief for which one the campaign is:
- **Purple Label** — men's luxury
- **Collection** — women's luxury

## Shared rule for both
Both inherit the Master Brand pillars (`master-brand.md`) but express them through craftsmanship-first storytelling rather than lifestyle-first — that's the key difference from Polo/Lauren/Home. **Never use the words "luxury" or "luxurious" in consumer-facing copy** — convey luxury without ever naming it. "Elegant" is also flagged as over-used for these two brands specifically (per internal creative-director feedback) — reach for a more specific word before defaulting to it.

## Collection (women's luxury)
**Overview**: RL's most glamorous vision of timeless luxury — a modern, elegant woman with chic feminine nonchalance, told through impeccably tailored clothes and a passion for artistry and craft. Never mentions trends or fashion. Frequent themes: classic menswear-tailoring, Old Hollywood glamour. Contains the evergreen **Icons Collection** capsule (seasonless foundation pieces).

**The story**: "They are all moods of the same woman enjoying her life and all the roles she can play... from day to night." — Ralph Lauren. Collection tells the story of the heroines who inspire him; tonally romantic and dreamy, never overwrought.

**Tone pillars** (shared structure with Purple Label, distinct execution):
- **ELEGANT** — refined, never forced or over-complicated; polished, poised, dignified.
- **EVOCATIVE** — exacting, precise adjectives that paint a nuanced picture while leaving the audience room to dream; never overly verbose.
- **ASPIRATIONAL** — elevated, invites the viewer into an exclusive world of power, elegance, beauty, ambition (again: never say "luxury").
- **TRADITIONAL** — rooted in old-world opulence, timeless, no reference to trends or cutting-edge fashion.

**Product naming/descriptives formula** (for PDP/lookbook copy, not email module copy): `[STYLE NUMBER] [COLOR] [SURFACE TREATMENT if any] [FABRIC, exact content] [ITEM NAME if applicable] [SILHOUETTE] [PRICE]` — e.g. *"790750633001 CLASSIC CHAIRMAN NAVY/GREY MÉLANGE EMBROIDERED WOOL-ELASTANE WARRINGTON COAT $5,995."* See `pdp-product-copy.md` for the full detail.

## Purple Label (men's luxury)
**Overview**: RL's men's luxury brand — confident and sophisticated, speaking elegantly about traditional craftsmanship, luxurious fabrications, and artful inspiration. Centers on craftsmanship, not lifestyle: the hand-stitched shoulder, the Mongolian cashmere washed and spun in Italy.

**The story**: Began 1995 with hand-tailored suiting inspired by Savile Row and Mr. Lauren's own bespoke English suits. "Purple Label was all about me... it's elegant and sophisticated. It says you have money, you are chic, you have style, you have arrived." Influences: Art Deco structure, Old Hollywood glamour, French Riviera ease.

**Tone pillars** (same four names as Collection, same discipline — apply to menswear/craft instead of womenswear):
- **ELEGANT**, **EVOCATIVE**, **ASPIRATIONAL** (never say luxury/luxurious), **TRADITIONAL** — identical definitions to Collection above, just pointed at tailoring, fabrication, and the "modern gentleman" instead of a heroine.

**Approved example (product description)**: *"Handcrafted in Italy, the Kent combines a lighter construction with fine canvassing and a soft shoulder to create a more natural profile."*
