# Master Brand — the umbrella every RL brand inherits from

Source: TAG's RL Master Tone & Voice Guidelines (Master Brand tab). Read this before any brand-specific file — the four Master Brand tone pillars underlie every sub-brand's own pillars, even when a sub-brand names them differently.

## The goal
Convey a beautiful, comfortable lifestyle while sharing a philosophy by which to achieve it.

## The story
Ralph Lauren's own life is the story of the American Dream — but the brand's storytelling has grown beyond that into a diversity of dreams, aspirational lifestyles, and emotional moments of happiness, triumph and togetherness, all built on the same archetypal themes from his own life. Wealth and power are not the focus: it's about achieving success on one's own terms, against one's *former self*, not against other people. The dream is democratic — "reserved only for those daring enough to take it."

## The narrator — a rule that affects every piece of copy
- **Brand copy** (captions, descriptions, everything except direct quotes): **third-person omniscient** ("We," "Us"). Ralph Lauren the man is the narrator and hero of the story, but the copy speaks *about* him and his world, not *as* him.
- **Quotes**: **first-person** ("I," "Me") — always genuinely in Ralph Lauren's own voice, sourced from real interviews/press (see the Quotes archive for ~150 sourced, approved quotes spanning 1974–2015 if a real quote is needed — never invent one).

## The tone
Simple, clear, concise — yet refined and romantic. Not overly verbose, not trying to impress. **The content of the story matters more than the flourishes used to describe the scene or setting.** Write the way Mr. Lauren speaks: quietly, with confidence, about experiences that inform and inspire — relatable, simple, authentic, clear. Each sub-brand takes on a different facet of that same voice (see the per-brand files), so the tone shifts brand to brand even though the underlying values don't.

## The four Master Brand tone pillars
- **SOPHISTICATED** — always elevated and refined; conveys luxury without being condescending, haughty, or out of touch. Noble, refined, dignified — yet simple and clear.
- **CONCISE** — "nothing is more beautiful than a complex idea expressed simply." Clear and direct; every word is carefully chosen but the presentation stays effortless. No room for misinformation or misinterpretation.
- **ROMANTIC** — romantic flourishes convey a mood, an emotion, a lifestyle — easy, simple, aspirational. Set the scene: the location, the characters, what they're doing, what they wear. Avoid piling on adjectives and adverbs; build the scene with simple, cinematic language instead.
- **CONFIDENT** — naturally confident, never egotistical or condescending. Empowered and inspiring. Elevated vocabulary, never didactic. **Always active voice — no passive voice.**

## The worlds / dreams — recurring thematic buckets across campaigns
Referenced across brands as inspiration buckets (a campaign or module often leans into one of these):
- **Adventure** (formerly "Safari") — spirit of travel and exploration, effortless timeless elegance
- **Bohemian** — the artist as muse; eclectic adornments, raw canvas, creativity and self-expression
- **Equestrian** — English-countryside estates, the grace and function of the equestrian tradition
- **New England Prep** — Ivy League classics, sporting uniforms, campus casual, reinterpreted
- **Old Hollywood Glamour** — sophisticated eveningwear, sharp tuxedos, silver-screen icons
- **Sport** — polo (the namesake), golf, tennis, regatta, equestrian arts
- **Automotive** — precision and style of the world's great cars (Mr. Lauren's own 1938 Bugatti collection is the reference point)
- **Western** — the cowboy, the campfire, the American West — freedom, individualism, authenticity

## Banned/avoid across all brands (see `sensitivity-and-compliance.md` for the full list)
"Timeless sophistication" and "our icon" are flagged buzz phrases; "luxury"/"luxurious" must never appear in consumer-facing copy (say it without saying it). Full banned-word list and reasoning is in `sensitivity-and-compliance.md`.
