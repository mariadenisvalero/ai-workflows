# Module types — the story-planning catalog

This is what this skill chooses *from* when turning a brief into a Campaign Plan. It's deliberately about shape and copy role, not pixels — see the Figma assembly skill for how each of these actually gets built.

## The fixed three-email structure

Every campaign now produces exactly three emails from one brief and one story. Unlike module count within a single email (still a judgment call, see below), **which three emails exist and their overall shape is fixed, not a choice**:

### Email 1 — Teaser
**Hero only.** No product modules, no "Shop Now" — this email's job is to introduce the story/season/collection and build anticipation, not to sell yet. Headline and body should read as an opening line, not a pitch. CTA (if any) is soft — "Discover the story," "Coming soon," "Stay tuned" — never a direct shop CTA; if the brief gives no natural soft CTA, it's fine for the Hero to close with just body copy and no CTA at all. Ends with the fixed Core App Banner + Navigation Footer pair (see below) — same as every email in the set.

### Email 2 — Product Focus
**Hero + 2 modules.** This is the actual shopping email — the one that does what a single RL email always used to do. Pick the Hero and the two modules' shapes the same way a standalone email's modules were always chosen (see "How many modules?" below, now scoped to exactly 2 beyond the Hero): read the brief's explicit structure first, then the image mapping's secondary/labeled selects, then decide from the shapes below. CTAs here are direct — "Shop Now," "Shop the Look." Ends with the fixed Core App Banner + Navigation Footer pair.

### Email 3 — Reminder
**Hero + one wrap-up module.** Closes out the story — recaps or highlights the collection, creates a "don't miss it" tone. The Reminder's Hero photo is allowed to be a different shot than Email 1's or Email 2's (a fresh angle on the same story, same palette) — it doesn't have to reuse either, though it can if that image genuinely fits best. The wrap-up module is typically a generic single-image module or a CTL-style pairing highlighting 1–2 more looks from the same collection, with a direct CTA back to shop ("Shop the Collection," "Don't Miss Out"). Ends with the fixed Core App Banner + Navigation Footer pair.

### Fixed element in all three: Core App Banner + Navigation Footer
Every one of the three emails ends with the same fixed pair — **`03 Banner/Core App Banner`** + **`01 Navigation Footer VWAP/Footer`**, banner above footer — staged same-file in the campaign template (see the assembly skill's `module-library.md`) — this isn't copy this skill writes fresh; just note in the Campaign Plan that it's included at the end of each email, the same way the other fixed brand modules below are handled.

## How many modules within Email 2 (Product Focus)?

No fixed formula beyond "exactly 2, plus the Hero." What decides the two modules' shapes:
1. **What the brief already specifies.** Some briefs spell out the exact structure (see `email-plan-format.md`'s worked example: "Hero - gif... Complete the Look... Bags... Accessories" — that's the brief handing you the plan almost fully formed). When the brief is this explicit, follow it.
2. **How many images the mapping actually supports.** A module needs enough distinct, appropriate images; don't invent a module the image mapping can't feed.
3. **What the story needs.** A single-product story might want two generic modules on different pieces; a fuller collection naturally wants a CTL/WIW pairing for the second module.

When the brief is vague on structure, propose a plan for Email 2's two modules and say so plainly — this is exactly the kind of judgment call the human expects to review and adjust, not something to quietly commit to.

## Reading the image mapping — check this before assigning anything yourself

Marketing briefs commonly split images into two pools:
- **Primary selects** — a pool of candidate images, generally for a Hero (any of the three emails) or for modules where the exact shot is still an open choice. You (or the human) pick from these.
- **Secondary selects** — images that arrive **already labeled** for a specific slot (e.g. an image explicitly named "Bags" and another named "Accessories" for a Complete the Look module). When an image is pre-labeled like this, that label *is* the module's structure — don't re-interpret it, just use it. These almost always belong to Email 2 (Product Focus), since that's the shopping-focused email.

Always check for secondary/labeled selects first. If they exist, they usually tell you the back half of Email 2's plan for free.

## Module shapes

### Hero (present in all three emails, exactly one each)
Media (image or gif) + headline + body copy + zero, one, or two CTAs depending on the email (see the fixed three-email structure above — Teaser's Hero is CTA-light or CTA-free, Product Focus and Reminder's Heroes carry a direct CTA).

### Generic single-image module
One image + a title (H2 or H3, brand-dependent — check the brand voice file) + optional short body + one secondary CTA. The default "tell me about this product/story" module when nothing more specific fits — usable in Email 2 or as the Email 3 wrap-up module.

### CTL — Complete the Look
Two images side by side, each labeled with its own short, underlined secondary CTA (e.g. "BAGS", "ACCESSORIES") — no shared headline/body copy the way a generic module has; the section eyebrow is usually just "Complete the Look." Each image's label is both its caption and its CTA. Use this in Email 2, or as the Email 3 wrap-up module when the brief pairs a hero look with 1–2 accessory/add-on items, or when the image mapping's secondary selects come pre-labeled this way.

### WIW — Wear It With
Similar spirit to CTL (styling-adjacent, multi-item), used when the brief frames it as styling suggestions around a core piece rather than a strict "complete the look" pairing. Confirm structure against the brief/image mapping the same way as CTL — don't assume it's identical every time.

### CYO — Create Your Own (Polo only)
Single image of a customized/personalized product + copy directly below the image + CTA directly below the copy. Structurally rigid: never separate the copy and CTA from being both right under the image.

### Fixed brand modules — copy is not written fresh
These exist as known building blocks; when a brief calls for one, just note that it's included — don't draft new copy for it:
- **Social Module** (any brand) — "Follow Us" + icon grid.
- **Store Locator Module** (any brand) — address/hours/map, not brand storytelling.
- **Sizes Modules** (Lauren) — Woman/Petite size-range banners.
- **Lauren Look Banner** (Lauren) — fixed rental-subscription promo.
- **Core App Banner + Navigation Footer** (any brand) — fixed pair, present at the end of all three emails in every set (see above).

## Worked example (real brief, for calibration — Email 2/Product Focus portion)
Brief: *"Utilize pre-fall site story for this email."* Then explicit structure: *"Hero - gif — Soft and tonal silhouettes create an effortless feeling for pre-fall — Shop Now. Complete the Look — Bags, Accessories."* Image mapping gave a pool of primary candidate shots (any could work for a Hero) plus two secondary selects explicitly labeled "Bags" and "Accessories."

Resulting Email 2 plan: **Hero** (image chosen from the primary pool, headline "Monochromatic," the body line straight from the brief, CTA "SHOP NOW") + **one CTL module** using the two pre-labeled secondary images, captioned "BAGS" / "ACCESSORIES." That's Email 2's two modules filled by the brief and mapping directly. Email 1 (Teaser) would then draw a different, story-introducing shot from the same primary pool for its own Hero, and Email 3 (Reminder) would pick one more shot (possibly a third primary-pool candidate, possibly reusing the Teaser's if nothing else fits as well) for its wrap-up.
